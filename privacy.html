Privacy Policy

Effective October 27, 2026 · Version 1.0

In short

  • Your information stays on your device, unless you choose to share it or back it up.
  • We don't collect, see, sell or share it.
  • No account. No ads. No tracking.
  • You can delete everything at any time.

1. Who we are

Tickwell is made by Hugo Xiaofeng Chen. We are responsible for this policy.

Privacy contact: tickwellpillpal@gmail.com

Canadian privacy laws apply to how we handle personal information, including the federal Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA).

2. What Tickwell stores on your device

  • Medicines you enter: name, dose as written (and dose steps, if the dose changes over time), times and instructions, when you pause and resume one, and a pill count if you choose to keep one
  • Exercises you enter: name, amount, times and instructions, the picture you pick for one, and when you pause and resume one
  • Visits and follow-ups: dates, clinic names, phone numbers, booking links and notes
  • What you check off or mark as skipped, with the time it was due and the time you tapped it, the time you say you took it or did it if you change it, how an exercise felt if you choose to say (easy, OK, hard or sore), any note you add, why you skipped it if you choose to say, and the times you log items you use only when needed
  • Photos you choose to add of a medicine (one) or an exercise (up to three). Tickwell makes them smaller and keeps them with your other information
  • Health stats you choose to log (blood pressure, heart rate, weight, temperature, blood oxygen, blood sugar, pain, sleep and water), with the time, any note, and the units and stats you choose to show
  • Steps: a step count for each day, which you type in or let Tickwell read from Apple Health, and a daily step goal if you set one
  • Your settings, such as whether reminders are on and whether they show names, Tick’s night sky (the stars he has lit and the days they were for), and the backgrounds you have earned and which screens use them
  • When you agreed to our Terms of Use and this policy, and which versions

This can include health information, which is sensitive. That's why it stays on your device.

3. Where it is stored

On your device, in the app's storage on your phone. If you turn on the weekly iCloud Drive backup, a copy is also kept in your own iCloud Drive (see section 4).

Tickwell keeps a second copy in the app's own storage on your phone, so your information isn't lost if your phone runs low on space. This copy also never leaves your phone, and "Delete all my data" erases it.

Tickwell is set so your phone's own backups (to the cloud or to a computer) don't include your Tickwell information. This keeps it on this device only. It also means that on a new phone, Tickwell starts empty, unless you restore a backup file you saved yourself, or your weekly iCloud Drive backup if you turned it on (see section 4).

It is not sent to us or to anyone else. We have no servers that store your information, and we cannot see it.

Tickwell does not use accounts, ads, analytics or tracking tools.

4. Information others may collect

  • App store: the app store you downloaded Tickwell from handles downloads and may collect information under its own privacy policy.
  • Links, phone numbers and directions: if you tap a clinic's phone number, booking link or Directions, your device opens another app or website (for Directions, a maps app or website with the address you typed). Their privacy policies apply.
  • Your calendar: if you tap Add to Calendar on a visit, you see your phone's own New Event screen. If you tap Add there, the visit's name, time, place and who it's with are copied into your calendar. That copy is kept by your calendar, under its own settings (for example, it may sync with your online calendar account). Tickwell never reads your calendar.
  • Notifications: reminders are set up on your device, using your phone's own notification system. The reminder text is kept by your device until it shows, and is not sent over the internet. By default it doesn't include names. If you turn on names, they may show on your lock screen.
  • Camera and photos: only when you tap "Add a photo" on a medicine or exercise, your phone asks if Tickwell may use the camera or your photo library. Tickwell keeps only the photo you take or pick, on your device. It never looks at your other photos. If you turn on names in reminders, a medicine's photo can show in its reminder, on your lock screen. When you tap "Scan the label" on the iPhone, the camera takes one photo of the label and your phone reads its words. That photo is not kept and nothing is sent anywhere. Only the words you tap go into the boxes, and you can change them.
  • Widgets: if you add a Tickwell widget, it shows your next times and today's progress, and item names only if you turn on names in reminders (never doses). This summary stays on your phone, is left out of backups, and is erased by "Delete all my data". If you use the widget's "Check off" button, the tap is saved on your phone until Tickwell opens and records it.
  • Apple Health (iPhone): only if you choose "Use Apple Health" in Steps and allow it in Apple's permission screen. Tickwell then reads your daily step count from Health, on your phone. It reads nothing else, never writes to Health, and never sends your steps anywhere. Steps it reads are kept on your device like the rest of your information. You can stop it in Steps, or in the Health app. Apple's privacy policy covers the Health app itself.
  • Siri and Shortcuts (iPhone with iOS 16 or newer): if you ask Siri "What's next in Tickwell?" or "Log my medicine in Tickwell", or use these in the Shortcuts app, Tickwell answers on your phone from the same summary the widget uses: times, and item names only if you turned on names in reminders, never doses. Logging asks you first and needs your phone unlocked. The tap is saved on your phone until Tickwell opens and records it. What you say to Siri, and Siri's spoken answer, are handled by Apple under its own privacy policy and your Siri settings.
  • Weekly update: "Send a weekly update" in Settings (Share with your care team) makes a short text message about your last 7 days (names of your medicines and exercises with how many you checked off or skipped, how many readings with the latest one, steps and water totals, and your next visit; never doses). You see it first, then your phone's share sheet opens and you choose the app and the person. Tickwell doesn't send it anywhere. Once it leaves your device, whoever receives it is responsible for it. An optional weekly reminder is set up on your device like other reminders.
  • Files you export: "Share with your care team" in Settings (My records) makes a PDF summary of your plan, readings and questions, or a spreadsheet file of every entry, on your device. "Visit summary" on a visit makes the same PDF. Each is handed to you. Tickwell doesn't send them anywhere. You choose where they go (for example, to save one or send it to your care team). Once a file leaves your device, whoever receives it is responsible for it, under their own privacy practices.
  • Backup files: "Save a backup file" in Settings (Your information) makes one file with all your Tickwell information, for moving to a new phone, and hands it to you the same way. Tickwell doesn't send it anywhere. You can choose to lock the file with a password. Locking happens on your device, and Tickwell never keeps or sends the password, so nobody (not even us) can open a locked file without it. A file saved without a password is not locked: anyone who has it can read your information. Either way, keep it somewhere private and delete it when you no longer need it. If you turn on "Remind me each month", Tickwell saves when you last made a backup file and reminds you on your device, like its other reminders. "Restore from a backup file" reads a file you choose, on your device, and replaces what Tickwell has on that device.
  • Weekly iCloud Drive backup: this is off unless you turn on "Save to iCloud Drive each week" in Settings (Your information), on an iPhone. Then, once a week when you open Tickwell, it puts the same backup file in its own folder in your iCloud Drive (Files, then iCloud Drive, then Tickwell), replacing last week's file. The file goes to your own iCloud account with Apple, under Apple's terms and your iCloud settings. It never goes to us, and we can't see it. This file is not locked with a password: anyone who can get into your iCloud account can read your information. You can turn it off at any time, and delete the file in the Files app. Turning it off doesn't delete the file.

5. Who can see your information

Anyone who can use your unlocked device may be able to open Tickwell and see your information. Use a passcode, fingerprint or face lock on your device.

If you share your device, others may see your information.

6. How long it is kept

Your information stays on your device until you delete it.

7. How to delete your information

  • In Tickwell, go to Settings and tap "Delete all my data".
  • Delete the Tickwell app from your phone.

Once deleted, it can't be recovered. We don't have a copy.

Backup files and exported files you saved, and the weekly iCloud Drive backup, are not in the app, so these steps don't delete them. Delete them yourself where you saved them (the iCloud Drive one is in Files, then iCloud Drive, then Tickwell).

8. Your rights

Under Canadian privacy law, you can ask an organization what personal information it holds about you, and ask it to correct that information.

We don't hold any of your Tickwell information. It is on your device, where you can see, change and delete it yourself. If you have questions, contact us.

9. Keeping it safe

Your information is protected by your device's security. Keep your device's software up to date and use a screen lock.

Because we never receive your information, we cannot recover it or remove it for you.

10. Children

Tickwell is not meant for anyone under 19 unless a parent or guardian agrees and helps them.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change how Tickwell handles your information, we will update this policy and its version and date, and ask you to review it in Tickwell before you continue.

We will not start collecting your information without telling you first and asking you to agree.
